Modeling Human Responses to Multimodal AI Content

Multimodal misinformation & human-centric AI

Zhiqi Shen† · Shaojing Fan† · Danni Xu · Terence Sim · Mohan Kankanhalli

National University of Singapore

† Equal contribution

github paper supp

Abstract

As AI-generated content spreads, so do risks of misinformation. While prior work has focused on detecting AI content, less is known about its influence on human perception and behavior. We introduce the MhAIM Dataset, containing 154,552 online posts (111,153 AI-generated), with 8,952 human annotations, enabling large scale analysis and model training of human responses. Our study shows that people are better at identifying AI content when posts include both text and visuals, especially when inconsistencies exist. We propose two composite metrics (trustworthiness and impact) for downstream risk assessment and decision support. We present T-Lens, an LLM based system that incorporates predicted human responses to multimodal information, with HR-MCP (Human Response Model Context Protocol) enabling seamless integration with any LLM. Our work provides insights and tools for equipping AI systems with human awareness capabilities, offering strategies to mitigate risks of AI driven misinformation.

Keywords: Intelligent agents; Social and Behavioral Sciences; Multimedia applications and multimedia signal processing.

Contributions

  • T-Lens — an agent system for human-aligned responses to multimodal content, centered on HR-MCP for predicting trustworthiness, impact, receptivity, beliefs, and sharing tendencies with interpretable explanations.
  • Human-study findings on sensitivity to AI-generated content across modalities, informing targeted mitigation strategies.
  • MhAIM — a large multimodal dataset of human- and AI-generated media with human response annotations for training and analysis.
